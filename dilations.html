Dilations on the Coordinate Plane

An interactive high-school geometry lesson

In this lesson, build a convex polygon, choose a center of dilation, and explore how the scale factor changes the figure. Positive, fractional, zero, and negative values of (k) show how a dilation can enlarge, reduce, collapse, or reverse a figure through its center.

Interactive dilation tool

Click grid intersections to add vertices of a convex polygon. After the third point, the tool arranges accepted vertices around the boundary automatically. Duplicate points and points inside the boundary are not added. Choose the center of dilation on the grid or enter its coordinates.

Distances from the original axes

These measurements are |x| and |y| from the original coordinate axes. When the center is not the origin, they do not generally scale by the same factor as the distances from the center.

Coordinates relative to the center

The x̂ and ŷ axes pass through the center and stay parallel to the original axes. The signed components show how far each point is from the center along those directions.

For a dilation, each signed center-relative component is multiplied by \(k\). The distance along an axis is the absolute value of that component.

Vertex coordinates

Compare each original vertex with its image as the coefficient and center change.

    What to notice

    For a point (P=(x,y)) and a center of dilation (C=(a,b)), the image is

    [ P’=C+k(P-C),x’=a+k(x-a),y’=b+k(y-b). ]

    • If (k>1), the image is enlarged and lies farther from the center.
    • If (0<k<1), the image is reduced and remains on the same side of the center.
    • If (k=1), the image matches the original polygon.
    • If (k=0), every vertex maps to the center.
    • If (k<0), the image lies on the opposite side of the center, with size determined by (|k|).

    Explore

    1. Build a triangle and compare its image for (k=2), (k=0.5), and (k=1). How does the distance from the center change?
    2. Try (k=0). Where do all the image vertices go?
    3. Try (k=-1), then (k=-2). How does a negative coefficient change the side of the center on which the image lies?
    4. Move the center away from the origin. Predict one image vertex, then use the graph to check your calculation.

    Learning goals

    • Identify the center and scale factor of a dilation.
    • Calculate image coordinates using a center other than the origin.
    • Describe how positive, fractional, zero, and negative scale factors affect a figure.
    • Compare a polygon with its image and explain which distances change by the scale factor.
