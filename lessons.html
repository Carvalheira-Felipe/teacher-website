Rotations on the Coordinate Plane

An interactive high-school geometry lesson

In this lesson, you will build a convex polygon on a coordinate grid, choose its center of rotation, and explore how each vertex moves as you adjust the angle from −360° to 360°.

Interactive rotation tool

Click grid intersections to add vertices in order around the polygon’s boundary. After the third point, the polygon closes automatically. Points that would make it concave or self-crossing are not added. Choose the rotation center on the grid or enter its coordinates.

Distances from the axes

Distance to the y-axis is |x|; distance to the x-axis is |y|. The dashed guides show these distances for each original vertex and its image.

With the center at the origin, 90° and 270° rotations swap the x- and y-distances, while a 180° rotation keeps them on their respective axes. If the center is elsewhere, distances to the coordinate axes are not generally preserved.

    What to notice

    A rotation turns every point through the same angle about a fixed center. In this tool, you choose the center \((h,k)\). Each vertex stays the same distance from that center, and the polygon keeps its side lengths and angle measures.

    For a counterclockwise rotation by θ degrees around \((h,k)\), transform each point using:

    x′ = h + (x − h) cos θ − (y − k) sin θ
    y′ = k + (x − h) sin θ + (y − k) cos θ

    Explore

    1. Plot a triangle, then rotate it by 90°. Compare the original and image coordinates. What pattern do you notice?
    2. Try 180°, −90°, and 270°. Which rotations produce the same image? Explain why.
    3. Rotate your polygon by 360° and −360°. What happens to each vertex?
    4. Predict the image of one vertex before moving the slider, then use the graph to check your prediction.

    Learning goals

    • Plot and identify vertices of a polygon on the coordinate plane.
    • Describe and model a rotation by its angle and center.
    • Compare coordinates before and after a rotation and explain how the transformation preserves distance and angle measure.
